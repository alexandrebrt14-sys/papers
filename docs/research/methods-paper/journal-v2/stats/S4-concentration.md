# S4. Concentration: how generative-engine visibility distributes across firms

Series 2026-04-23 to 2026-09-08. Canonical stratum `COALESCE(is_probe,0)=0`, 68,624 observations, 127 cohort entities of which 111 name a real firm and 16 are fictitious decoys. Generated by `s4_concentration.py`; every figure below is interpolated from that script and none is transcribed by hand.

## Method

Every observation is read under the uniform 200-character window, that is over `response_text[:200]`, with the project's own extractor (`src.analysis.entity_extraction.EntityExtractor`) over the v2 cohort including international anchors and fictitious decoys, reached through `../tables/_common.py`. Holding the matching rule fixed and moving only the window is the premise of the manuscript, so this section imports the rule rather than restating it. The identity check reports 0 mismatches: on every row whose stored text is at most 200 characters, re-extraction reproduces the stored `cited_v2` exactly. A non-zero count would have aborted the run.

Two size variables are reported throughout and they answer different questions. **Mentions** counts an entity once per observation in which it appears anywhere inside the window, which is coverage. **First mentions** counts an entity only when it is the earliest cohort name in the window, which is the leading position. First mentions reproduce Table 8 of the manuscript to every published digit, and that agreement is the check that this script reads the series the same way `build_tables.py` does.

Three denominators appear, and every table names the one it uses. The **real cohort** is the 111 entities that name an existing firm, 79 Brazilian and 32 international anchors; it is the primary denominator because the sixteen decoys cannot be cited correctly and counting them as zeros measures the study design rather than the market. The **full cohort** adds those sixteen. **Named only** restricts to entities with at least one mention and is reported because the published Gini of Table 8 uses it.

Intervals on the concentration indices come from a cluster bootstrap over observations, 2,000 replicates, seed 20260911: an observation is the unit that was sampled, an entity is not, and resampling entities would treat a fixed cohort as a random draw. Intervals are percentile.

## 2. The shape of the upper tail

These data cannot tell a power law from a lognormal, and the design is what stops them: the Kolmogorov-Smirnov rule leaves 19 entities in the fitted tail, and at that size the two families fit equally well by construction. The tables below report the fits in full so that a reader can see how far short of a determination they fall.

**Table S4.5.** Power law against lognormal on the entity mention counts. Discrete maximum likelihood, xmin chosen by minimising the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance (Clauset, Shalizi and Newman). Goodness of fit is the CSN bootstrap over 1,000 synthetic datasets; the power law vs lognormal comparison is the normalised log-likelihood ratio with its two-sided Vuong p-value.

| Power law vs lognormal, R | -0.0091 | -0.3622 |

| Power law vs exponential, R | 1.8031 | 2.2610 |

*Notes.* Read the third row of this table before the fifth. The KS rule places xmin at 246 mentions, which leaves 19 entities in the fitted tail out of 66 with any mention at all and 111 in the cohort. An exponent estimated on 19 points carries a standard error of 0.281, so the interval [1.674, 2.776] covers most of the range that any tail model would produce. The bootstrap goodness of fit does not reject the power law (p = 0.9570), and the likelihood-ratio comparison against lognormal returns R = -0.0091 with p = 0.9927. A normalised ratio that close to zero is the numerical statement that the two families fit the same data equally well. The fitted lognormal is itself degenerate, with mu = -287.2 and sigma = 15.5: the likelihood is flat along a ridge, which is what happens when a two-parameter family is asked to describe 19 points.

The honest conclusion is that these data do not distinguish the families. A power law is not rejected; neither is a lognormal; the exponential is the only candidate the comparison pushes against (R = 1.8031, p = 0.0714), and even that falls short of conventional significance. The constraint is structural, and collecting for longer does not lift it: the cohort has 111 real firms by design and only 66 were ever named, so the largest sample any amount of further collection can put into a tail fit is a few dozen points. Distinguishing a power law from a lognormal at these sample sizes is not a question the design can answer, and the right way to report it is to say so rather than to quote alpha = 2.23 as though the family were settled.

The last three rows of the table are a second estimator written for this section against scipy, following the same Clauset-Shalizi-Newman recipe: maximum likelihood for the exponent with the Hurwitz zeta normaliser, xmin by minimum KS distance over the candidate values, and the same bootstrap for goodness of fit. On the mention counts it lands one candidate value away from the library, xmin 261 against 246, with alpha 2.174 against 2.225. On first mentions the two agree on xmin 47 and on alpha to 0.0000, and differ on D because the two implementations break the discrete step at the tie differently. A criterion whose optimum shifts under a difference that small is flat near its minimum, which is the same finding the likelihood ratio reports and rules out an implementation artefact as the source of the ambiguity.

Rank-size pairs for anyone who wants to plot the distribution directly are in `data/s4_rank_size.csv`.

## 5. How many firms an answer names

A single overdispersed process accounts for the whole distribution, zeros included. The negative binomial beats the zero-inflated forms on both information criteria and reproduces the observed zero count to within 55 answers out of 68,624, so the excess of zeros over Poisson needs no second mechanism to explain it. Citing is rare and clumped: most answers name nobody and the ones that name anybody tend to name several.

Adding engine, vertical and language as covariates does not change the ranking: Poisson AIC 79,330, negative binomial 76,349, zero-inflated negative binomial 76,351. The dispersion parameter is what changes across those specifications, and the ordering of the four families does not.

The as-collected column is reported beside the windowed one because the two differ by construction, and what separates them is the window. Perplexity is the only arm whose stored text exceeds 200 characters: 7,435 of its 7,741 canonical rows, mean stored length 668 characters. Its answers name an entity in 5,796 rows as collected and 4,028 inside the window. Every count comparison across arms therefore has to state its window, which is why this section uses the uniform one everywhere else.

## 6. Where the first mention falls

The six engines reach their first cohort entity at medians spanning 0 to 129 characters, a spread covering most of the observation window. On the rows that retained the whole response the same engines cannot be told apart once the offset is expressed as a fraction of the answer, which makes the absolute spread a joint statement about where an engine starts naming and how long it writes. A protocol that reports position has to declare the window before the number compares across engines.

### The relative offset is censored for five of the six arms

A relative offset, the position of the first mention as a fraction of the answer, is the quantity one would prefer to compare, and for most of this series it cannot be computed. Five client adapters stored `response_text = text[:200]`. For those arms the denominator is the constant 200 whenever the answer reached that length, so the relative offset is the absolute offset rescaled and adds nothing to Table S4.14. It also carries a bias in a known direction: the answer ran past the cap, so offset divided by 200 overstates how late in the text the first name appears, and the overstatement grows with the length of what was discarded. ## What this does not establish

**Concentration is measured, its cause is not.** Every index here describes the distribution of names across answers. Nothing in the design separates an engine that concentrates because the market is concentrated from one that concentrates because its training corpus over-represents the same few firms, or because the query wording invites a single answer. The battery contains directive prompts that ask for the best or the leader, and those prompts mechanically produce one name; the section does not decompose the indices by query type, and until it does no share of the observed HHI can be attributed to engine behaviour rather than to the instrument.

**The tail family is undetermined, and no amount of further collection settles it.** With 19 entities above the selected xmin, the likelihood-ratio comparison against lognormal returns R = -0.0091. Nothing here licenses the sentence "citation follows a power law", and the exponent of 2.23 should not be quoted without the sample size that produced it.

**Never named is never named in this window, in this battery, by these engines, in this period.** The 45 real firms with no mention were not tested against queries naming them. A firm can be absent from an answer to "which company leads Brazilian retail" and be described accurately when asked about directly; the adversarial probe stratum exists for that question and is excluded here by `COALESCE(is_probe,0)=0`. Absence of coverage in a discovery battery is not absence of knowledge.

**The anchor comparison is not a test of home bias.** The two groups differ in size (8 against 19 or 20 per vertical), in tier composition (all anchors are head, the Brazilian set spans three tiers) and in what the query asks for, since every prompt names Brazil. The per-slot rate handles the size difference and nothing handles the other two. The language interaction in Table S4.9 is the finding that survives those caveats, because it compares each group with itself across languages.

**The count models describe the marginal distribution and do not license a causal reading of the zeros.** The negative binomial beating the zero-inflated forms says that one overdispersed process fits; it does not say that no distinct abstention mechanism exists. Abstention was measured directly in the refusal taxonomy of the manuscript, and this section neither confirms nor contradicts it.

**Offsets in Table S4.14 are offsets inside a window.** For five of the six arms the answer beyond character 200 was never written to disk, so no re-extraction can recover it, and a first mention that fell past the cap is absent from the distribution rather than late in it. Table S4.15 carries the uncensored measurement on 2,225 rows from the end of the series, and on those rows the engines separate on absolute offset and not on relative offset. A reader who needs relative position should use Table S4.15 with its sample sizes and its period, and should treat the level as specific to that slice.

**Matching leaks are declared and not corrected.** Two cohort names collide with ordinary words and two more are nested inside another cohort name. The sensitivity analyses in Tables S4.4 and S4.11 bound their effect; correcting them belongs in `src/config.py` followed by re-extraction, which would break the correspondence with the stored series that the identity check establishes.
